# Public event or travel: the 72-hour check

The aim is not secrecy. It is to avoid publishing a ready-made schedule and to prepare for predictable transitions.

## 72 hours before

- Review public announcements, speaker pages and social posts.
- Remove unnecessary transport, hotel and precise timing details.
- Decide who really needs the full itinerary.
- Check whether personal and professional accounts expose the same trip.
- Name one contact for security or welfare concerns.

## 24 hours before

- Confirm transport, arrival and venue contacts through trusted channels.
- Agree how urgent requests will be verified while the person is traveling.
- Identify staffed places near key transitions: reception, station desk, venue security or police point.
- Share the minimum useful itinerary with the right internal contact.

## During the event or journey

- Post location updates after leaving, not while present.
- Avoid displaying badges, boarding passes, room numbers or access details online.
- Be attentive at transitions: home to transport, arrival, hotel, venue and return.
- Treat an unusual encounter as an observation, not proof.
- If immediate danger is suspected, move to a populated staffed place and contact local emergency services.

## Within 24 hours after

- Remove temporary access and shared itinerary links.
- Record unusual contacts, lost items or suspicious requests.
- Check whether photos or posts exposed new details.
- Brief the appropriate security contact when several observations connect.

Escalate when there is repetition, corroboration or a direct link to the person's role, access, family or route.
