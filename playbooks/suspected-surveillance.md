# Suspected surveillance: a safe response

A strange observation is not proof. The priority is safety, factual reporting and corroboration — not confrontation.

## If danger may be immediate

1. Do not confront or try to identify the person yourself.
2. Move toward a populated, staffed place such as a reception desk, shop, station office or police point.
3. Contact local emergency services and follow their instructions. In France, call **17** or **112**.
4. Tell a trusted contact where you are.

## Record facts

As soon as it is safe, write down:

- date, time and exact location;
- what you directly saw or heard;
- direction of travel;
- a neutral description of people, vehicles or objects;
- who else was present;
- why the observation stood out;
- any lawful supporting material already available.

Use the [observation log](../templates/observation-log.md). Separate facts from interpretation.

## Look for corroboration

One coincidence may mean nothing. Escalation becomes more important when there is:

- repetition across different places or times;
- the same distinctive person, vehicle or behavior;
- an unusual contact followed by a digital request;
- a link to a public event, trip, privileged role or recent exposure;
- independent confirmation from another person or authorized source.

## Preserve and report

- Keep original messages, images and notes.
- Do not edit files or publish accusations online.
- Report through the organization's security, legal or management channel.
- Share only with people who need the information.
- Let trained or authorized people decide whether further investigation is required.

Do not change routes or test suspected followers merely to confirm a theory. That can increase risk and produce unreliable conclusions.
